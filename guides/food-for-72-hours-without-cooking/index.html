What food to store for 72 hours without cooking?

Choose long-life food you can eat straight from the pack: canned meat, fish, vegetables and fruit, crackers, bars, nuts, dried fruit and peanut butter, plus a manual can opener and utensils. Ready.gov, BBK and Poland's RCB all point to food that needs no refrigeration, cooking or special preparation.

What to choose: ready to eat, no fridge needed

Ready.gov advises stocking canned foods, dry mixes and other staples that do not require refrigeration, cooking, water or special preparation. Its suggested list includes ready-to-eat canned meats, fruits and vegetables, protein or fruit bars, dry cereal or granola, peanut butter, dried fruit, canned juices and high-energy foods. It also suggests comfort foods.

Germany's BBK gives similar examples: dried fruit, canned fruit, vegetables, meat and fish, dried meat or sausage, nuts, rusks and muesli bars. BBK explains the logic. Food that is already cooked is useful when the stove stops working in a power cut. Food that keeps without cooling is useful when the fridge and freezer fail.

In Poland, the government's Safety Guide (MSWiA, MON and RCB) lists canned food, rusks and bars as examples for power outages, as reported by the media. An RCB spokesperson also said food that needs no heat treatment, such as tinned or jarred meals, is the key thing to stock.

Check that it really needs no cooking

Dry rice and pasta are good pantry staples. As one summary of the Polish guide points out, they still need water and a way to prepare them. For a strict 72-hour no-cook set, make sure most meals can be eaten cold, straight from the pack.

Ready.gov says to include a manual can opener and eating utensils. The same Polish summary suggests small packs so that a whole portion gets eaten once it is opened. It also suggests sketching a simple meal plan to see whether the stock will last your household three days.

Tailor it to your household and keep it fresh

Ready.gov advises choosing foods your family will actually eat and remembering special dietary needs. That includes infants. For allergies, intolerances and children, pick products that suit each person.

RCB guidance, as summarised in the media, is to keep food in a cool, dry, dark place. Use the items with the shortest dates first and replace them with new ones. Ordinary supermarket food you eat anyway is enough, so you don't need specialist products.

FAQ

Is it mandatory to store 72 hours of food in Poland?

No. Media reports on the government's Safety Guide stress that it is a recommendation. There is no legal duty to keep a set amount of food, water or cash at home.

Can I count rice and pasta in a no-cook supply?

Only if you can still prepare them. They need water and a heat source, so for a strict no-cook plan rely mainly on canned goods, crackers, bars, nuts, dried fruit and similar foods.

What else should go with the food?

Ready.gov says to add a manual can opener and eating utensils. Also check the foods suit everyone's diet, including infants and anyone with allergies.
